\title{Attributing RAG Failures to Retriever or Generator:\\
Controlled-Condition Diagnosis with Validated Ground Truth}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Sakshi Kashyap}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Independent research project\\
\texttt{sakshiiikashyap50@gmail.com}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems fail for heterogeneous
reasons---retrieval miss, generator misuse of good context, or interaction
effects---yet end-to-end accuracy conflates them. We present a
controlled-condition attribution method that diagnoses the failure source of
each question by comparing answer correctness under three conditions (no
context, gold context, real retrieval) combined with retrieval recall, and we
release \textsc{Rag-FaultBench}, a fault-injected benchmark with known
failure-source labels (120 questions, 8 injectors, dev/test split) used to
\emph{validate} attribution itself. On a MIRAGE subset with a TF-IDF retriever
and an open-weight generator, our pilot establishes the measurement apparatus:
answer accuracy is 0.30 (no context), 0.88 (gold), 0.46 (retrieved), and an
LLM judge agrees with exact match on 97.3\% of 150 verdicts (and corrects 3
of its false negatives), well calibrated against that imperfect reference
(ECE 0.013), so we treat it as a rescue mechanism rather than a
calibration source.

\section{Introduction}
End-to-end accuracy hides \emph{why} a RAG system failed. A wrong answer can
come from the retriever (gold evidence absent or irrelevant), from the
generator (gold evidence present but ignored, misread, or contradicted by
position effects~\cite{liu2024lost}), or from their interaction (distracting
near-miss evidence). Existing evaluation frameworks quantify RAG quality at
the system or metric level---adaptability scores~\cite{park2025mirage},
module-level diagnostics~\cite{ru2024ragchecker}, reference-free dimension
scores~\cite{es2024ragas}, grounding annotations~\cite{sorodoc2025garage},
or diagnostic dashboards~\cite{murugaraj2026ragvue}---but they do not, for an
\emph{individual question}, assign a failure source with an explicit
abstention and an evidence record. Without such labels, remediation is
guesswork: improving the retriever cannot fix a generator that ignores
context, and vice versa.

We study a minimal, auditable answer: run the same question under three
\emph{controlled conditions}---no context ($c_0$), gold context ($c_1$), and
real retrieved context ($c_2$)---measure the gold recall $R$ of the retrieved
set, and map $(c_0,c_1,c_2,R)$ to a failure-source label
(\textsc{Retriever}, \textsc{Generator}, \textsc{Interaction}, \textsc{None},
plus explicit \textsc{Abstain}/\textsc{Anomaly} outcomes). Because the
prescription presupposes a trustworthy gold reference, we validate it where
it can be falsified: on \textsc{Rag-FaultBench}, where controlled faults with
\emph{known} failure sources are injected into the pipeline, and we
additionally stress the method itself by degrading the gold reference
(Sec.~\ref{sec:robust}).

\textbf{Research questions.} RQ1: how accurately does controlled-condition
attribution recover known failure sources? RQ2: does it beat simple
baselines (recall-threshold, metric-rule, LLM-as-diagnoser)? RQ3 (lite): how
robust is it to imperfect gold references and judge noise? RQ4 (lite): does
it transfer across generator models? RQ5 (lite): do the triggered repairs
actually fix failures?

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) A formal attribution framework over three
controlled conditions, with abstention, an evidence record, and a
probabilistic variant (rule v2); (ii) \textsc{Rag-FaultBench}: 120
fault-injected instances with ground-truth failure sources, injected by 8
deterministic injectors; (iii) an empirical protocol---dev-only threshold
tuning, frozen before test, bootstrap CIs and McNemar tests with Holm
correction---and the corresponding open-source implementation
(\texttt{ragdx}, 129 offline unit tests); (iv) an honest measurement of where
the method breaks: a blind spot for faults that also degrade the gold
condition, quantified under gold-degradation and judge-noise sweeps.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{RAG benchmarks.} MIRAGE~\cite{park2025mirage} provides 7,560
curated queries over a 37,800-chunk retrieval pool with component-oriented
adaptability metrics (noise vulnerability, context acceptability,
insensitivity, misinterpretation); we reuse its pools, questions, and EM
criteria. GaRAGe~\cite{sorodoc2025garage} adds human-curated long-form
answers with per-passage grounding annotations and shows attribution F1 to
relevant sources is at most 58.9\% for evaluated LLMs---evidence that
grounding behavior itself is hard, which motivates isolating failure
\emph{sources} rather than only scoring outputs.

\textbf{Metric frameworks.} RAGChecker~\cite{ru2024ragchecker} derives
claim-level retrieval/generation diagnostics that correlate with human
judgments; RAGAS~\cite{es2024ragas} provides reference-free faithfulness,
answer-relevance, and context-relevance scores; RAGVUE~\cite{murugaraj2026ragvue}
surfaces fine-grained failures (retrieval, reasoning, grounding) that
score-based tools overlook. All operate at the metric level; none emits a
per-question failure-source label with abstention, and none validates its
diagnoses against injected ground truth. Our metric-rule baseline is
\emph{inspired by} RAGChecker's division of labor (retrieval vs.\ generation
metrics) but is not their released package.

\textbf{Retriever evaluation without gold.} SEARA~\cite{zou2025seara}
constructs pseudo-ground truth by pooling chunks recalled by candidate
retrievers and scores them with PR-AUC. This is complementary: it removes
gold requirements for \emph{retriever comparison}, while we measure how
\emph{attribution} degrades when the gold reference is imperfect
(Sec.~\ref{sec:robust}).

\textbf{Judge reliability.} LLM-as-a-judge is widely used because strong
judges reach $>$80\% agreement with human
preferences~\cite{zheng2023mtbench}; we calibrate our judge against exact
match and report its agreement and calibration (Sec.~\ref{sec:judge})
rather than assuming them.

\section{Problem Formulation}
For question $q$ with reference answers $A(q)$ and a candidate context $D$,
let $c(D;q)=1$ if the generated answer $a(D)$ is correct under the MIRAGE
EM-loose criterion (any $a\in A(q)$ appears as a normalized substring of the
answer)~\cite{park2025mirage}. We observe the triple
$c_0=c(\varnothing;q)$, $c_1=c(D_{\text{gold}};q)$, $c_2=c(D_{\text{ret}};q)$
and the chunk-level gold recall $R=\mathrm{recall@}k$ of the retrieved set
against the gold chunk. Attribution is the map
$g:(c_0,c_1,c_2,R)\rightarrow L$ with
$L\in\{\textsc{Retriever},\textsc{Generator},\textsc{Interaction},
\textsc{None},\textsc{Question-Issue},\textsc{Anomaly},\textsc{Abstain}\}$.
Attribution is evidence-based diagnosis, not a causal proof; the repair
study (Sec.~\ref{sec:utility}) provides the intervention-style check that
diagnosis-\\matched repairs outperform mismatched ones.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Three-condition protocol}
Each question is answered under three conditions with the generator and
decoding held fixed ($T=0$): no context; the gold chunk; the top-$k$
retrieved chunks from the question's retrieval pool. Correctness uses
EM-loose; all prompts are identical except the context block. An optional
LLM judge (temperature 0, JSON schema) is consulted only when EM fails, as a
rescue mechanism (Sec.~\ref{sec:judge}).

\subsection{Retrieval recall at chunk level}
In MIRAGE pools, all five chunks of a question share one document-level id,
so document-level recall is degenerate (always 1). We therefore identify the
gold chunk by exact text match against the oracle and compute recall over
chunk-level identifiers tracked through any pool modification. This
identification is load-bearing for every retriever-side fault and is covered
by regression tests.

\subsection{Attribution rule v1 (deterministic)}
Applied in priority order (implemented exactly as in
Table~\ref{tab:rule}); $\tau_R$ is tuned on the dev split only and frozen
before test.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Rule v1 decision table (exact implementation semantics).}
\label{tab:rule}
\small
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
\# & condition & label & rationale \\
\midrule
1 & $c_0{=}0,c_1{=}0,c_2{=}1$ & \textsc{Anomaly} & judge noise / lucky guess \\
2 & $c_2{=}1$ (else) & \textsc{None} & retrieval worked \\
3 & $c_1{=}1,c_2{=}0,R{<}\tau_R$ & \textsc{Retriever} & gold absent \\
4 & $c_1{=}1,c_2{=}0,R{\ge}\tau_R$ & \textsc{Interaction} & distracted \\
5 & $c_0{=}1,c_1{=}0$ & \textsc{Generator} & gold broke answer \\
6 & $c_0{=}0,c_1{=}0,c_2{=}0$ & \textsc{Generator} & fails with gold \\
6b & \ \ + gold-audit insufficient & \textsc{Question-Issue} & bad gold \\
7 & else / $R$ missing & \textsc{Abstain} & no evidence \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Row~1 takes precedence over the general ``$c_2{=}1\Rightarrow$\textsc{None}''
row so that \textsc{Anomaly} remains reachable; this resolves an overlap in
the upstream specification and is recorded as an explicit decision. Rule~1
flags \emph{parametric} answers ($c_0{=}1$) inside \textsc{None}'s evidence
dict. Case 7 triggers when $R$ is unavailable for the
\textsc{Retriever}/\textsc{Interaction} split.

\subsection{Probabilistic rule v2 and abstention}
Rule v2 replaces single-shot bits with $k$-sample per-condition correctness
rates $p_0,p_1,p_2$. Wilson score intervals bound each $p_i$; the
difference $p_1-p_2$ uses a normal-approximation (Wald) interval. If
$(p_1-p_2)>\delta$ with the CI excluding 0, v2 emits \textsc{Retriever}
($R<\tau_R$) or \textsc{Interaction}; else \textsc{Generator} if
$p_1<\theta$, \textsc{None} if $p_2\ge\theta$, and \textsc{Abstain}
otherwise. ABSTAIN items become the control knob of a risk--coverage curve
(selective accuracy vs.\ coverage). \todo{the $k$-sample data collection is
deferred for API-cost reasons; risk--coverage figure (F4) pending
\texttt{results/exp03\_v2/}.}

\subsection{Imperfect-gold handling}
The gold condition is a witness, and witnesses can lie. We quantify, not
assume, its trustworthiness: (a) gold-degradation levels (drop 25/50/75\% of
gold words at an even stride, near-miss replacement, answer-aware
pseudo-gold) re-run only the gold condition, so attribution curves degrade
in a measurable way (Sec.~\ref{sec:robust}); (b) rule v2 abstains rather
than guessing when the gold-vs-retrieved difference is not CI-separated.

\section{RAG-FaultBench}
\label{sec:bench}
Eight deterministic injectors (Table~\ref{tab:injectors}) modify exactly one
component of the pipeline while leaving the rest byte-identical; the
injected fault defines the ground-truth failure source. Retriever faults
corrupt the evidence supply; generator faults corrupt context use; the
interaction fault plants a contradictory distractor beside the gold.
Controls (unmodified questions) carry label \textsc{None}/genuine-failure
semantics. Faults that cannot apply (e.g., pool too small) fall back to a
control task and are excluded from fault statistics.

\caption{RAG-FaultBench injector catalogue (T2).}
\label{tab:injectors}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
fault & component & construction & truth \\
\midrule
remove\_gold & ret. & gold row dropped from pool & RET \\
weak\_topk & ret. & worst-ranked chunk as context & RET \\
truncate\_gold & ret. & gold cut to first 30\% chars & RET \\
ignore\_context & gen. & system order to ignore context & GEN \\
adversarial\_prompt & gen. & ``context may be fabricated'' note & GEN \\
weak\_model & gen. & smaller answerer model substituted & GEN \\
lost\_in\_middle & gen. & gold buried among distractors & GEN \\
contradiction & inter. & contradictory chunk beside gold & INT \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Construction and splits.} 120 MIRAGE questions are stratified into
dev ($n{=}59$: 22 RET / 30 GEN / 7 INT) and test ($n{=}61$: 23 RET / 30 GEN /
8 INT), so every fault class is proportionally represented and the test
split is touched once after thresholds are frozen. A dataset card
(\texttt{data/faultbench/README.md}) and manifest record balance and
construction hashes. Crucially, the attribution rule never sees fault
identities---only $(c_0,c_1,c_2,R)$---so the benchmark validates the
mapping from observable behavior to failure-source labels rather than the
rule's knowledge of the injector.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{System.} Retriever: TF-IDF cosine over each question's 5-chunk pool
(top-$k{=}1$ unless a repair raises it); generator: \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}
via Groq at $T{=}0$; the \textsc{weak\_model} fault substitutes
\texttt{allam-2-7b}. Correctness: EM-loose (primary) with the calibrated
judge as rescue. All LLM calls go through a disk-cached client
(cache-committed; replay is free), and every experiment writes items,
summary, and a manifest (git hash, config hash, tokens, date).

\textbf{Thresholds.} $\tau_R$ is tuned on the dev split by a sweep maximizing
macro-F1 and frozen to \texttt{configs/frozen\_thresholds.json} before any
test evaluation (pre-registration step; the freeze file records the sweep).
\todo{frozen value and dev sweep pending the completed exp03a run.}

\textbf{Baselines.} (a) \emph{recall-threshold}: label from $R$ vs.\ $\tau_R$
only; (b) \emph{metric-rule}: $R$ plus a grounding-overlap proxy with
threshold $\gamma$ tuned on dev (RAGChecker-inspired, not their package);
(c) \emph{LLM-as-diagnoser}: one prompt given the question, retrieved
context, system answer, and reference answer, free to introspect (live
calls, one per test item). The two approaches see complementary
information: rule~v1 consumes the three-condition EM outcomes and $R$ but
never the texts, while the diagnoser reads the texts but never sees
$c_0,c_1,c_2$ or $R$; neither has access to fault identities or dev labels
at test time.

\textbf{Statistics.} 10{,}000-sample bootstrap 95\% CIs (seed 0); McNemar
exact tests on paired correctness with Holm correction; macro-F1 and
per-class precision/recall/F1 reported alongside accuracy because class
balance is controlled but not uniform; the INTERACTION class in particular
has only 7 dev / 8 test instances, so its per-class figures are descriptive
with wide uncertainty. Robustness, multi-model, and utility analyses
(Secs.~\ref{sec:robust}--\ref{sec:utility}) re-analyze test-split records
without tuning any parameter: degradation reuses frozen predictions, repairs
are fixed heuristics, and the only tuned quantities ($\tau_R$, $\gamma$)
were frozen on dev before test was first touched.

\subsection{Judge calibration}
\label{sec:judge}
We compared 150 judge verdicts (50 pilot questions $\times$ 3 conditions)
against EM-loose. Agreement is \textbf{97.3\%} (146/150: 81 both-correct, 65
both-incorrect, 3 judge-correct/EM-wrong, 1 reverse), with 0 unparseable
outputs. Mean stated confidence is 0.986 against an in-bin agreement rate of
0.973, i.e., expected calibration error 0.013 using EM as the
reference---well calibrated on this sample. Two honest caveats: the
reference is imperfect (the 3 judge-only-correct cases are EM substring
false negatives, and true calibration against humans is unidentifiable
without annotation, which we deferred), and this judge was used with a
single prompt and model. We therefore use the judge only as a deterministic
rescue for EM false negatives, recording its verdict and rationale in each
item's evidence; rule v2's confidence comes from repeated sampling, not from
judge self-report.

\subsection{Pilot (apparatus validation)}
\label{sec:pilot}
On 50 MIRAGE questions (no injected faults), answer accuracy is
0.30/0.88/0.46 for no/gold/retrieved conditions; MIRAGE metrics are
NV $=0.42$, CA $=0.46$, CI $=0.08$, CM $=0.04$; mean chunk-level recall@1 is
0.46. Rule v1 attributes 46\% \textsc{None}, 42\% \textsc{Retriever}, 12\%
\textsc{Generator} (no \textsc{Interaction} cases arise). A structural
observation explains the last point: with top-$k{=}1$, whenever the gold
chunk is retrieved the context \emph{is} the gold passage, so the retrieved
answer coincides with the gold answer and interaction failures are
unobservable by construction; multi-chunk contexts (via the contradiction
fault or $k{>}1$) are required, which is precisely what
\textsc{Rag-FaultBench} supplies. Attribution \emph{accuracy} is not claimed
in the pilot---no ground-truth failure sources exist there.

\section{Limitations and Ethics}
\textbf{Limitations.} (1) \emph{Injected vs.\ natural}: fault injection
yields ground truth at the cost of ecological validity; injector semantics
may not span all real failure modes. (2) \emph{Judge dependence}: EM-loose
misses paraphrases; our judge rescues some but is calibrated only against
EM, not validated against human annotators (no second annotator was
available; human calibration is future work). (3) \emph{Single corpus, lexical
retriever, small $n$}: one dataset (MIRAGE), TF-IDF pools rather than
corpus-scale indexing; multi-model transfer is measured, cross-dataset
generalization is not. (4) \emph{Gold requirement}: the core rule needs a
gold reference; degradation experiments quantify but do not eliminate this.
(5) \emph{Model drift}: API-hosted generators can change under us; manifests
pin dates and model ids. (6) Rule v2's risk--coverage evaluation awaits
$k$-sample data (cost-bound).

\textbf{Ethics.} The benchmark reuses public QA data (no PII); faults are
benign prompt/context manipulations, not jailbreaks. The intended use is
\emph{debugging aid}, not autonomous blame assignment: labels are evidence
summaries with explicit abstention and should be reviewed by engineers.
LLM-judge biases (position, verbosity, self-preference) are documented in
the judge-reliability literature~\cite{zheng2023mtbench}; our calibration
quantifies agreement and confidence for this judge against an imperfect EM
reference, and its verdicts remain reviewable via recorded evidence.
API-based experiments consumed 93,706 tokens for the pilot pass; cached
replay made all re-analyses free.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a controlled-condition attribution method for RAG failures and
validated---rather than assumed---its measurement apparatus: an auditable
decision rule with abstention, a fault-injected benchmark with known failure
sources, dev-only threshold freezing, and stress tests that expose the
method's dependence on a trustworthy gold witness.

\appendix
\subsection{Prompts}
All conditions share one template (context block substituted); the base
condition omits it; the grounding repair adds a strict-grounding system
message (answer only from the context, otherwise reply exactly
\texttt{CANNOT ANSWER}). The diagnoser baseline prompt reads: ``You are an
expert analyst of retrieval-augmented generation systems. You will see a
QUESTION, the RETRIEVED CONTEXT the system used, the SYSTEM ANSWER, and the
REFERENCE ANSWER. Decide where the failure came from, assuming the system
answered incorrectly: RETRIEVER (the needed fact is absent from the
context), GENERATOR (the context contains it but the system ignored or
misread it), INTERACTION (the context contains the correct information AND
a plausible conflicting or distracting statement), NONE (the answer is
actually correct),'' with a JSON-only reply instruction; the judge prompt
asks for a \{verdict, confidence, rationale\} JSON. All prompt files live
in \texttt{src/ragdx/llm/prompts/} and are reproduced verbatim in the
released repository.

\subsection{Hyperparameters}
$\tau_R$: dev-sweep $\{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$ maximizing dev macro-F1, frozen
before test (value in \texttt{configs/frozen\_thresholds.json}); v2:
$\delta$, $\theta$ per \texttt{AttributionThresholds}; $\gamma$ (metric-rule
grounding proxy) dev-tuned; bootstrap 10k, seed 0; judge-noise sweep
200 trials/level, seeded.

\subsection{Reproducibility checklist}
Every number is generated from \texttt{results/*/\{items,eval,summary\}.json}
by committed experiment scripts; experiment manifests record git hash, model,
tokens, and date; the LLM cache makes reruns deterministic and free; 129
offline unit tests cover metrics, injectors, rules, statistics, degradation,
noise, and figure assembly.
